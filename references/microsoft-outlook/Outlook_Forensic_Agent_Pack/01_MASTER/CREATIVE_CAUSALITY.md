# Counterfactual creative causality and taste
The seventh pass reached a subjective 95% estimate of conceptual readiness after user calibrated 94%. This is not a causal experiment. Alternative decisions have not been rendered or tested. Treat the following as design hypotheses to critique and validate.
## Essential relationships vs replaceable aesthetics
Retain coherent spatial hierarchy, material differentiation, controlled optical depth, local alignment, negative space, functional clarity, scale-dependent detail, independent object roles and coordinated motion. Replace Microsoft blue, logos, typography, email content, specific stationery, exact geometry and wallpapers with original product-specific assets. Never rely on literal substitution alone.
## Counterfactuals and roles
| Observed choice | Alternative | Likely loss / rule |
|---|---|---|
| Translucent curved layered rails | Solid straight uniform paths | Less optical continuity and directional flow, greater obstruction; architecture must do multiple jobs. |
| Textured sphere amid planar objects | Another smooth cuboid | Less geometric/tactile contrast and distinct secondary motion; choose missing qualities. |
| Loose wire clips | Perfectly aligned clips | Less familiar scale and local irregularity; globally ordered, locally varied. |
| Flexible email sheets | All rigid rectangles | Weak physical correspondence metaphor and semantic transformation; material behaviour communicates meaning. |
| Fine grid | Blank floor or high-contrast grid | Less scale reference or too much competition; detail appears when useful. |
| Restrained camera while reading | Constant orbit | Reduced readability, less contrast between spectacle and comprehension. |
| Neutral UI | Saturated every surface | Expressive accent scarcity lost; colour follows visual responsibility. |
| Delayed sphere and staggered avatars | Simultaneous entry | Competing movement and less discernible progression. |
| Cropped peripheral props | All objects fully contained | World feels like a bounded composition, less discovery. |
| Small warm organic accent | No accent or large decorative object | Less emotional punctuation, or excessive competition with CTA. |
## Five hierarchies
Spatial: environment → architecture → interface → component → micro. Material: neutral substrate → structural colour → optical accent → expressive accent. Motion: camera → primary action → supporting action → settle. Information: product → feature → control → state → feedback. Focus: peripheral atmosphere → context → subject → active interaction. All dynamically vary; complexity peripheral in discovery, central in demonstration.
## Emotional hypotheses
Confidence through stable composition, clear alignment and decisive transitions. Intelligence through semantic reflow and meaningful transformations. Precision through grid, fine edges, organised libraries. Approachability through rounded forms, familiar objects and soft illumination. Playfulness through expressive contrast and selective overshoot. Calmness through negative space and stillness. Sophistication through optical differentiation and scale detail. Reliability through persistent identities and functional states. Discovery through occlusion and progressive revelation.
## Deliberate exclusions
Avoid constant orbital motion, uniform gloss, excessive universal emission, oversized decorative text, random floating props, perpetual particles, identical ease curves, heavy texture on functional panels, persistent dramatic perspective and decorating every object. Exceptions are acceptable for expressive moments when justified. Not a categorical ban. Evaluate each against readability, visual economy and narrative.
## Creative audit (every hero object)
1. Semantic necessity: product meaning or narrative role?
2. Geometric contribution: missing form or useful contrast?
3. Spatial responsibility: alignment, boundary, hierarchy, scale or connection?
4. Material contribution: appropriate tactile/optical quality?
5. Perceptual priority: correct contrast, size and focus?
6. Motion necessity: what changes in understanding?
7. Temporal responsibility: what leads/follows/holds?
8. Scale resilience: meaningful at wide and close views?
9. Emotional contribution: reinforces desired experience?
10. Economy: what can be removed without loss?
11. Continuity: belongs to system, not random prop?
12. Counterfactual strength: why this choice over plausible alternative?
Use the answers as design review evidence, not a mechanical checkbox score. Do not claim artist intent. If two choices fulfil the same role, compare in actual renders during production.
